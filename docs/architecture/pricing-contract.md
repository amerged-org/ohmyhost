# PRICING.md

This file is the single source of truth for what ohmyho.st charges and how usage becomes a credit deduction. `PLAN.md` owns the roadmap, `ARCHITECTURE.md` owns the design; neither restates prices. A price that is not in this file does not exist. A number on the website, in a Skill or in an API description must equal the number here, and the number here must equal the rate card in code.

## Credits

- One credit has a nominal sale value of USD 0.01. One credit is 1,000,000 microcredits. All arithmetic is integer (`bigint`) on microcredits and USD micro-units, except the AI cost, which is exact in integer nano-USD ([AI usage](#ai-usage)); displayed rounding never changes a charge.
- The approved rate formula is `credits = direct_provider_cost_usd × 1.15 / 0.0035`. USD 1 of provider cost is about 328.571 credits. In code this is the exact rational 2300/7 credit micro-units per USD micro-unit (`packages/platform/src/shared/usage-pricing.ts`).
- PGCF database memory is a fixed retail tariff independent of allocated infrastructure cost: `credits = memory_mib_seconds / 92160`. Included volume and seven days of database backups have no additional charge. Missing provider cost remains unknown.
- Mail is another explicit exception: each sent recipient and each received message costs 0.18 credits (180,000 microcredits), based on USD 0.0009 variable Resend cost plus 100% markup at the nominal USD 0.01 credit value. Provider subscription/domain fees are not allocated; webhook retries add no mail charge. The send charge is taken when the request is claimed and given back once if the provider rejects or refuses the send; after the provider accepted it, a later delivery failure does not refund it.
- Every rate rounds up (`ceil`) to the next microcredit. No rate has a minimum charge.
- Provider allowances (free tiers, included quotas) are platform pools, not customer entitlements: a customer pays the list-cost rate from the first unit.
- Each organization holds one credit account with two amounts. Monthly credit (the Free allowance or the Paid invoice allowance) ends with its period and does not roll over; one-time credit (top-up, automatic recharge, signup bonus, referral reward) never expires. A charge takes monthly credit first, then one-time credit ([credit account](billing-and-pricing.md#credit-account), [D445](decisions/billing-and-usage.md#d445)).
- A balance never goes negative. Usage that the available credits cannot fund is recorded as platform overrun, never as customer debt.

- Volume bonus (25% more credits per dollar for the portion above USD 100): `credits = usd ≤ 100 ? usd × 100 : 10,000 + (usd − 100) × 125`, so 10 packs grant 10,000, 11 packs 11,250, 20 packs 22,500 and 100 packs 122,500 credits. The credit grant of a payment follows this rule. A refund or dispute takes the granted credits back in proportion to the returned money, never more than the grant. Consumption rates never change with purchase size.
- Monthly credit is the Free allowance or the Paid invoice allowance with that period’s powered-by bonus. It ends with its period without rollover: the UTC month for Free, the paid invoice period for Paid. A paid invoice replaces the monthly credit, so unused Free credit ends when a paid period starts. A paid invoice for a period that a Paid renewal already bridged nets what that renewal spent of the allowance: the period's 1,000 credits are granted once in all, so 900 credits spent in the bridge leave 100 of it. Credit is spent monthly first, then one-time. One-time credit (top-up, automatic recharge, signup bonus, referral rewards) never expires, also after a downgrade to Free; this replaces the earlier rule that top-ups ended with the Paid coverage.
- Only active Paid workspaces may buy top-ups or enable automatic recharge. A Stripe renewal that is not paid keeps Paid for up to 14 days after the paid period ends: `paid_until` is the period end plus 14 days, and the monthly credit that renews inside that window is the Paid allowance, ending with the window or the month, whichever comes first. A renewal paid within that window continues coverage; otherwise the workspace returns to Free and renews with the Free allowance. A subscription that Stripe ends, such as a cancellation at the paid period end, closes the Stripe coverage at that instant, without the 14 days. Without Paid access automatic recharge starts no refill; the saved consent stays until the Owner turns it off. Signup and referral credit keep their own terms.
- Paid unlocks a customer-owned domain, verified transactional mail and AI model calls as capabilities. Their consumption is still metered.
- A new workspace starts Free. Paid access comes from a Stripe subscription, a referral’s 30-day grant, or access an operator set on the account; signup and campaign credits never grant it.
- The opt-in "Powered by ohmyho.st" flag is a small link on a project's production site. While the current owner shows it, a Free workspace may connect its own domain to that project, and each new period paid through Stripe adds 250 credits once per workspace, however many projects show the flag (granted/referral Paid days add none) (1,250 for USD 10, 25% more monthly credits). The flag does not waive the hostname price: the credit service bills every active custom hostname per finished day like any other. The flag links to `https://ohmyho.st/?r=flag-<project>` and counts as a referral while the project still shows it.
- Every workspace has its own referral link `https://ohmyho.st/?r=ref-<workspace>` (REST, CLI `referral link`, MCP `referral_link_get`, the portal's "Refer and earn" chip). A new user whose first workspace comes from a referral or flag link receives 1,000 credits and 30 days of Paid access; that workspace's first payment gives the referring workspace 1,000 credits and 30 more days of granted Paid access, unless an active Stripe period or an unbounded grant already covers it. We never buy or register domains.
- Automatic recharge is off by default, needs explicit Owner consent, and is capped by a gross UTC-month limit (default USD 100, configurable USD 10 to USD 1,000). It never combines with a volume bonus.
- Prices are identical in the US and EU hosting regions. Ordinary project creation defaults to US; the approved PGCF transition can change project metadata to US while existing R2/Git/artifact locations remain at their recorded locations. Region does not change a rate.
- There is no fixed project-count limit. Every project draws from the organization's one credit account. A project may set a monthly budget with mode `continue` (default) or `stop`; a budget is a limit over that account, not a second balance.
- Stripe owns money, invoices, tax and refunds. The Core's credit account owns credits; every change is one logged batch keyed by its Stripe or usage identity ([billing](billing-and-pricing.md)). A refund or dispute takes back the original grant in proportion to the returned money. A fully returned subscription invoice also ends that invoice's Paid coverage, and a partial return leaves Paid as it is.

## PGCF database sizes

The approved PGCF tariff is implemented in source; activation is prospective and requires the cutover/release evidence in W01. Historical Neon charges and their immutable cards remain unchanged.

| RAM     | Included nominal volume | Credits per awake hour | Availability  |
| ------- | ----------------------- | ---------------------: | ------------- |
| 256 MiB | 500 MiB                 |                     10 | Free and Paid |
| 512 MiB | 1 GiB                   |                     20 | Paid          |
| 1 GiB   | 2 GiB                   |                     40 | Paid          |
| 2 GiB   | 4 GiB                   |                     80 | Paid          |
| 4 GiB   | 8 GiB                   |                    160 | Paid          |

Volume includes PostgreSQL, WAL and filesystem overhead. Seven days of database backups and the allocated volume remain included while asleep. Sleeping databases have no memory runtime charge; CPU, connections and database transfer have no separate customer meter. These terms do not change customer file-storage or on-demand export contracts.

`pgcf.memory.active` uses exact `memory_mib_milliseconds` under `pgcf-memory-2026-10-26-v1`; the integer microcredit charge rounds up once per line (project, environment and meter) of each completed hour. Thus 256 MiB for 100 awake hours costs 1,000 credits and 512 MiB for 30 awake minutes costs 10 credits. Billing begins at the first complete UTC hour after the database started serving; the transition hour is waived. Resource changes charge only observed applied size. A missing/nonfinal usage interval is pending, never zero. A smaller nominal volume needs a separate migration; loss of Paid does not silently shrink existing storage.

### Scheduled and webhook executions

Schedule runs and webhook delivery attempts have no price of their own. They are readable as counters per hour or day (`database executions`, `database_execution_usage_get`). What a schedule or a webhook costs is the awake compute time of the database, which the memory meter `pgcf.memory.active` charges. A run or a delivery that finds the database asleep wakes it, and that awake time is charged like any other.

### Build price

A build runs in one Cloudflare build sandbox of instance type `standard-3` (2 vCPU, 8 GiB memory, 16 GB disk). The provider charges USD 0.00006112 per second (2 × 0.00002 + 8 × 0.0000025 + 16 × 0.00000007), which the formula turns into 0.020083 credits per second; the rate is exact in code as 3.5144 credits per 175 seconds.

A deployment plan quotes the build before it starts: 840 reserved seconds (14 minutes) = 16.86912 credits (shown as USD 0.168692). Accepting the deployment reserves the quote in the same batch that accepts it, provided the available credit covers it and a `stop` budget has room for it; a build has no grace period and never starts on credit that is not there. Settlement charges the measured seconds, from the sandbox start the Build Sandbox Durable Object accepted to its observation of the end (a few seconds after the sandbox's own end: the next 5 s poll plus the status read), rounded up to the full second, at the quoted rate to the project, and releases the rest of the reservation. A build that ran longer than the reserved seconds is charged for every measured second. The time the sandbox needs to start is not billed, and the sandbox's own timestamps are never billed. A build that never started, because it failed before launch, its sandbox never started or its operation was abandoned, releases the whole reservation; a started or uncertain build keeps it until its measured evidence arrives. An instance type without a published price is an error, never zero usage.

## Live Preview source candidate

The owner-selected W41 preview rate uses `wall_ms × 21120 + cpu_ms × 20000` pico-USD, then the existing exact credit conversion and ceiling. Dev and Prod previews use the same formula. Only awake VM time is metered; sleeping time has no compute charge and backup storage is included. The rate is a source candidate until the release activates its meter and rate card; a missing active price fails admission closed.

One VM run has one fixed provider label and a rolling credit hold. A physical stop records trusted Worker wall duration and releases the hold immediately; it does not invent a CPU value. The first actual CPU observation charges the wall duration and cumulative CPU; later larger provider totals charge only their positive CPU difference. ## Browser PDF

The W47 Browser PDF service is an explicit exception to the general provider-cost formula: **published variable provider cost × 2**, converted at the nominal USD 0.01 per credit. No additional general platform margin applies. Its meter is `browser.duration`, unit `millisecond`. At Cloudflare's reviewed USD 0.09/hour, 1,000 measured milliseconds cost 25 USD micro-units and 5,000 microcredits: 18 credits/hour, with no per-request minimum. Provider subscription fees and account-wide included allowances are not allocated to individual calls.

Both operator publication and the Core require non-null provider cost and `credit_micros = provider_cost_micros × 200`; the units-per-charge denominator preserves fractional per-millisecond cost exactly. Cards are immutable and activated prospectively under the normal price rules. The reservation pins its card/activation/rate, so an in-flight request never switches price when another card becomes active.

Only the provider's reported browser duration is settled; missing or invalid measurement is labelled unmeasured and charged zero. Known zero remains zero. Measured failed or late provider work is settled once under the original call, including after an orphan sweep; a 30-second client timeout is not a guarantee of provider cancellation. Stop budgets prevent new admissions rather than undoing already admitted work. HTML/PDF are not billing records. Published/activated card readback and actual provider usage parity remain W47 acceptance gates; this source table is not evidence of activation. [Cloudflare pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/browser-run/pricing/), [service boundary](services-and-workers.md#browser-pdf-gateway).

## AI usage

Model calls through the platform's AI gateway are sold on the same prepaid credit. They are an explicit exception to the formula above, like mail: the price is the factor below, not the conversion 1.15 / 0.0035 of the formula above. Its meter is `ai.cost` with unit `usd_micro`.

- The AI card requires prospective activation of `ai.cost`. Until a card is in force every call is refused with `ai_unavailable` (503); a call is priced by the card in force when it is settled.
- Paid workspaces only. A workspace without active Paid status is refused before the call.
- The price of a call is the cost orq.ai recorded for the call, times 1.045 (orq's credit fee), times 1.10 (the platform margin), times 1.21 (a further 21 % decided by the owner on 2 November 2026). The factor is 1.390895: 1,390,895 microcredits per 10,000 USD micro-units of cost, that is 139.0895 microcredits per USD micro-unit. Example: a call of 3.6 USD micro-units of cost (3,600 nano-USD) is charged 501 microcredits.
- The cost of a call is the cost orq.ai recorded for its trace, read from telemetry (`cost.total` filtered by trace id), in integer nano-USD (1e-9 USD). The factor applies to that recorded cost and the charge rounds up once to a whole microcredit. A recorded zero is charged zero; an observed zero is retained through later unavailable telemetry attempts while a later positive value still takes priority. On 3 November 2026 the owner approved [D452](decisions/billing-and-usage.md#d452): only answered `/v1/chat/completions` and `/v1/responses` calls with no usable cost after the bounded lookup use a USD 0.01 cost fallback before the same factor (1,390,895 microcredits at the current card). It is not a minimum for measured costs. The settlement records `usage.fallback_nano_usd: 10000000` and logs `llm_cost_fallback`. Other routes retain unavailable-cost settlement at zero with `cost_unmeasured`; the platform absorbs those unknown costs. There is no automatic later correction, speech character tariff or realtime duration tariff. A failure before an upstream answer releases the hold without a charge.
- Before the call, credit is held as a fixed hold of USD 0.50 of provider cost, which is USD 0.695 of credit at the factor, so a workspace needs about USD 0.70 of available credit per call in flight. The hold is settled with the recorded cost and the unused part returns to the account. A call that costs more than the hold is charged in full. A call that fails before orq returns an answer is released and not charged. A hold still open after 15 minutes is released by a sweep.
- An organization holds at most 8 unresolved AI calls at once (an open credit hold of an admitted call). A ninth call is refused before anything is reserved with `ai_rate_limited` (429, `Retry-After: 5`). The cap is checked in the same batch as the hold, so concurrent calls cannot pass it together; a settled or released call stops counting at once.
- A realtime voice session (`GET /v1/realtime`, a WebSocket) is admitted and held like a call. It ends after five minutes or sixty seconds idle. Settlement follows the recorded trace cost under the same rule as every other modality, including recorded zero and unmeasured-cost reconciliation; connected duration is not a separate charge.
- A project budget with mode `stop` refuses further calls once usage and reservations reach its limit. AI usage counts against the project budget like any other usage.
- There is no separate AI tier, minimum on a reported cost or additional per-request fee. The conditional LLM missing-cost fallback above is the only assumed cost.
- The gateway offers exactly the AI routes of the spec (chat completions, responses, image generations and edits, audio transcriptions and speech, decisions, OCR, realtime voice, and the model list), one to one: every model orq.ai serves on these routes is offered. The platform has no model catalog and no price table: reported model costs take priority; only the explicitly documented LLM missing-cost fallback substitutes an assumed cost. The environment's jurisdiction setting may refuse some models, which is an access rule and not a price rule.
- The Core charges AI calls synchronously when each call is settled, like builds and mail ([how usage becomes a deduction](#how-usage-becomes-a-deduction)). The AI factor and card identity are prepared in this release commit. Publishing this table to the website and documentation requires the matching immutable card to exist first; activation remains prospective.

## How usage becomes a deduction

Builds, mail and AI calls charge synchronously in the Core. The credit service bills the other four sources in finished windows: Worker usage and PGCF memory per UTC hour, R2 and scripts/custom hostnames per finished UTC day. Each source has a cursor; a window is billed once, oldest first, and a source with no cursor is not billed.

| AI call                      | the cost orq.ai recorded for the call's trace x the factor 1.390895                                              | held at admission as a fixed USD 0.50 of provider cost, at most 8 unresolved per organization, settled with the recorded cost when the call finishes, released when it fails before an answer or is swept after 15 minutes | seconds                                                                              |

| Mail received                | the platform's receipt of the message                                                                            | charged once per message, in the batch that marks it billed                                                                                                                                                                | about a minute after receipt                                                         |
| Worker requests and CPU      | Cloudflare Logpush usage objects in the private usage bucket                                                     | credit service, passes at minutes 27 and 57; hourly windows five minutes after they end, only `fetch` events with outcome `ok` or `exception`                                                                              | up to about 90 minutes                                                               |
| PGCF                         | final native hourly memory rollups                                                                               | credit service, passes at minutes 27 and 57; an hour is final two hours after it ended and the provider needs about twenty minutes more to finalize every database; only databases one customer owned for the whole hour   | at least two hours after interval close, usually about 2.5 hours after the hour ends |
| R2 storage                   | the Core's confirmed file peak of the UTC day                                                                    | credit service, per finished UTC day (one hour after midnight)                                                                                                                                                             | up to 25 hours                                                                       |

| Scripts and custom hostnames | the Core's receipts: staged scripts not cleaned up, hostnames provisioned not deleted                            | credit service, per finished UTC day: active scripts and active hostnames, each times 86,400 seconds at the per-second price of the card                                                                                   | up to 24 hours                                                                       |

- Usage is posted by the Core as one batch per organization and window under the key `usage:<source>:<organization>:<window start>`, so a repeated posting changes nothing. A window of an organization with more than 2,000 lines (about 1,000 environments) is booked in parts, each one batch under the same key with `:p<part>` added for a part above 0; a window is booked either whole or in parts, never both, and a posting that fails is kept with its lines and booked by a later pass, so one organization's failure never delays another organization's charges for longer than a few passes ([usage billing](billing-and-pricing.md#usage-billing)). It is priced by the rate card in force at the window start, rounded up once per line, and never repriced afterwards.
- The hostname meter has no waiver: a custom hostname is billed per finished day on every plan, with or without the powered-by flag.
- A rate card must be published before its effective time. A meter charges only after a separate, prospective activation, and a later activation of the same meter replaces the earlier one from its own start time. A window is priced by the activation in force at its start.
- Published rate cards and meter activations are configuration rows of the Core that an operator sets outside the product runtime with `pnpm credit:prices` ([changing prices](billing-and-pricing.md#changing-prices-and-adding-priced-features)); the credit service reads them and cannot write them. A published card and an activation are immutable; a published price alone never starts billing, and an activation is prospective.
- Runtime probe windows and unattributed or platform-scoped provider usage are platform expense.

## When credits run out

- A build whose quote the available credit cannot cover is refused with `insufficient_organization_credits`; a project `stop` budget refuses with `project_budget_exceeded`. A build has no grace period. Mail may still send during the grace period.
- The account records the instant its credit reached zero. From it runs one durable seven-day grace period per organization: existing services and domains keep running, but a month's platform overrun at its ceiling ends serving, Paid features and mail sends at once. A grant or a renewal clears it. The deadline is published through the API, SDK, CLI and MCP, and the Owner receives one warning mail per exhaustion episode.
- After an unresolved credit grace period, unfunded execution and mail are suspended; the credit grace and the fourteen-day unpaid-renewal window of Paid coverage remain distinct. An explicit Stop budget pauses both Dev and Prod once usage plus reservations reach the limit, without waiting for credit grace. The pause covers scheduled function runs: the scheduler claim applies the same serving decision as HTTP, a paused cron does not run or catch up, and it fires again at its next occurrence once credit or budget returns. Data, diagnosis, cancellation, buying credit and export stay available.
- A failed or unperformed provider action is never charged. Platform-caused retries and outages are platform expense. A billing-check outage alone never stops authorized application traffic.
