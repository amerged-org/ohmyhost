## D442 Fixed PGCF memory tariff and separate infrastructure cost

<a id="d442"></a>

Status: **Implemented in source; prospective meter activation and live acceptance pending** (W01, 2026-10-26; the billing path was rebuilt by W33).

PGCF costs 10 credits per 256 MiB of configured RAM per awake hour, proportional to exact native active time. Nominal volume and seven days of backups are included; asleep memory costs zero and no additional database CPU, connection or transfer meter applies. Native observations retain millisecond precision (exact MiB-milliseconds) and round once at monetary valuation in the credit service. A final hour without a usable memory quantity is skipped, never priced as zero, and an hour in which a database that bills is not finalized by the provider yet is not billed yet, while a never-final row of a database that no longer bills in that hour holds nothing back and is only counted. Customer charging starts at the first complete UTC hour after the database began serving. Serving intervals attribute delayed final hours to the owner and logical environment of that hour; shared physical data charges once at project scope. The tariff is independent of the platform's node cost; the authoritative amounts and size table live in the [price contract](../pricing-contract.md#pgcf-database-sizes).

## D445 One credit account per organization

<a id="d445"></a>

Status: **Implemented and rolled out to Development and Production on 30 October 2026 (W33, owner order of the same day); the first log rotation and the first PGCF-hour billing are still to be observed** (steps in the cutover runbook, record). No history is migrated and no compatibility path is kept.

Decision. The Core database holds credit amounts, not the story of how they came to be. One `credit_account` row per organization carries a monthly credit that expires with its period, a one-time credit that does not, the reservations of open builds, the platform overrun, the exhaustion time and the Paid coverage; `project_credit` carries each project's month-to-date use and optional budget; `credit_reservation`, `credit_grant`, `usage_cursor` and the day's `credit_log` complete the six tables of migration 0600. Every change of an amount is one D1 batch that also inserts a `credit_log` row under a UNIQUE key, so a retry, a replayed event or a concurrent writer cannot post twice and no version fence or lock is needed. The reason for each change is that log row; at the first run after 00:00 UTC, once every usage cursor has passed midnight, the private `credit-service` Worker archives the day's rows to the R2 bucket `ohmyho-credit-log-<environment>` in 256 shards, verifies them and deletes them from the Core; a lifecycle rule removes the objects after 14 days, and the customers' usage report is the last 14 days read from those objects plus today's rows.

Usage is billed hourly by the credit service (Worker requests and CPU from the Logpush files, PGCF memory hours, R2 storage and operations and the scripts and custom hostnames per finished day); mail and builds charge synchronously in the Core (mail at claim with a refund when the provider rejects, builds reserved at accept, settled by measured seconds, released when never started). The serving decision of the gateway reads the account and the project's budget: no spendable credit after the seven-day grace denies, and a `stop` budget denies at its cap.

Rule change. One-time credits (top-up, auto-recharge, signup, referral and bonus credit) do not expire; only the monthly Free or Paid allowance ends with its period. This replaces the earlier rule that top-ups ended with the Paid coverage. The public contract lost the `spent`, `expired` and overrun totals of the credit read, and `credit-usage` returns the last 14 days by day, project and meter without month or cursor parameters.

Removed. The lot, hold, effect, valuation, adjustment and wallet-version chain with its projection and expiry caches, the metering families, owner epochs and the private Metering Executor and Scheduler Workers, the collection cursors and claims for PGCF and R2, the Logpush importer and its Queue notifications, and the operator tools of the PostgreSQL Control. Migration 0601 drops 88 tables, 215 triggers and 6 views. Superseded decisions: D101, D366 and D433; D252 lost its operator command. Balances survive approximately (migration 0600 seeds them from the valid lots), and reservations of builds that ran during the cutover do not.

Prices. Rate cards and meter activations stay the only price state, both append-only. Migration 0602 re-keys `usage_meter_activations` by `(meter, starts_at)`, because the original key `meter` allowed one activation per meter and so no price change; the card of a meter's latest activation that started by an instant prices that instant, for the usage sources (`readRates`) and for mail alike, and a published card that no activation names prices nothing. The operator publishes and activates with `pnpm credit:prices` (dry run by default, one atomic batch of INSERTs, custody journal, read-back, `--confirm production`): a price never applies retroactively, a published card never changes and the unit of an activated meter never changes. An activation cannot be cancelled; a mistake is corrected by a later activation. See [changing prices](../billing-and-pricing.md#changing-prices-and-adding-priced-features).

## D452 LLM missing-cost fallback with recorded-cost priority

<a id="d452"></a>

On 3 November 2026, after ten successful text-model probes with ten readable Orq cost values, the owner explicitly approved actual LLM cost with the earlier USD 0.01 fallback only when no usable cost is reported. This supersedes unmeasured-zero charging only for `/v1/chat/completions` and `/v1/responses`. Reported zero remains zero, even through later unavailable lookups; a later positive value within the existing retry window wins. No measured cost is raised to a floor.

The gateway applies 10,000,000 nano-USD only after an answered LLM call has no trace or all bounded cost reads are unusable. Existing numeric usage carries `fallback_nano_usd: 10000000`; the safe log outcome is `llm_cost_fallback`. The Core applies the unchanged active card and atomic/idempotent reservation settlement. There is no schema, RPC shape, rate card, hold, cap or permission change and no automatic later correction. Refusals before an upstream answer release the hold. Speech, images, transcription, OCR, decisions and realtime retain their existing unmeasured-zero rule. On 15 November 2026 a realtime session stopped being unmeasured by construction: each upgrade carries a gateway-generated W3C `traceparent`, because orq records the session cost under the trace id it receives, so the session settles at the cost orq records for it. A cost not readable within the polling window still settles at zero and is logged as `cost_unmeasured`; no realtime duration tariff exists.

Specification, task plan. Source `778535d7e57bcb92b4322374f423a6a292881364` passed complete exact-source verification and CI `41712537052`, then released to Development and Production. ## D490 Platform overrun ceiling of USD 50 per organization and month

<a id="d490"></a>

**State:** implemented in source; release pending (Core migration [0670](../../../apps/control-store/migrations/core/0670_credit_overrun_month.sql)). **Context:** Overrun is booked in `overrun_micros` and never resets, so a monthly ceiling is needed to bound what the platform absorbs for one organization. **Decision:** the renewal stores the overrun as it stood at the month's start in `overrun_base_micros`; the month's overrun is the difference, and at USD 50 (5,000 credits) with no spendable credit left the serving decision, AI admission, mail sends and Paid product admission (Paid features, Paid compute sizes, plan changes and builds) deny with `insufficient_organization_credits` and the hourly sweep disables the schedules ([D498](#d498)). **Consequence:** existing accounts start with their whole overrun as base, so only overrun booked after the migration counts; the ceiling caps platform absorption and grants no credit, and it ends at the next renewal.

## D491 Referral credit for the first twenty users of one link

<a id="d491"></a>

**State:** released to Development and Production on 14 November 2026. **Context:** Credit and granted Paid access for every user of one referrer link would let that link collect without a bound, so the first twenty recorded users alone qualify. **Decision:** completion counts the users recorded through the same origin no later than the completing user, ordered by `recorded_at` and then user id; the credit and the Paid access are granted only while that rank is at most twenty, and later users get the workspace alone. **Consequence:** a late completion keeps its place in recording order; the count is of recorded signups, not of completions, so a user who signed up early and never completed still holds a rank.

## D492 Ten referral rewards per referrer organization

<a id="d492"></a>

**State:** released to Development and Production on 14 November 2026. **Context:** A referrer earns one reward per paying organization, which needs a ceiling for each referrer. **Decision:** before a reward is granted, the referrer's `referral_reward:` grants, reversed ones included, are counted in the same batch as a guard; at ten the reward is refused as `precondition_changed` and the payment completes without a reward. **Consequence:** a reversal does not free a slot, and the eleventh and later payers of that referrer earn nothing without an error to the payer.

## D493 Clawback of the referral reward with the earning payment

<a id="d493"></a>

**State:** released to Development and Production on 14 November 2026. **Context:** A referrer keeps a reward unless the payment that earned it is returned, so the reward has to follow that payment. **Decision:** a refund or a lost dispute takes the reward back only when it is the payment that earned the reward: the payer's paid grant made at the reward's instant, or the latest one before it. The reward's one-time credit is reversed, and the thirty granted Paid days it added come off the referrer's access, never before now; the Paid days are guarded by the reward not being reversed yet, so overlapping reversals subtract them once. **Consequence:** a payment that came before the referrer was active earned nothing and reverses nothing; a refund of a later payment changes no referral state.

## D494 Output-token hold for JSON model calls

<a id="d494"></a>

**State:** released to Development and Production on 14 November 2026. **Context:** A fixed hold ignores the requested output size, so a long answer can exceed it and a short one holds as much as the longest. **Decision:** only `/v1/chat/completions` and `/v1/responses` read an output-token limit (`max_tokens`, `max_output_tokens` or `max_completion_tokens`, the largest named value, 1 to 16,384, anything else refused before any hold). Their hold is USD 0.0001 per requested token, never below USD 0.50, and a call that names no limit is held as 16,384 tokens. Multipart and realtime calls and the other JSON routes keep the fixed hold. **Consequence:** the largest hold of one call is about USD 1.64, and the organization-wide cap of eight unresolved calls is unchanged.

## D495 Upload claims hold quota until resolved

<a id="d495"></a>

**State:** released to Development and Production on 14 November 2026. **Context:** An abandoned signed upload must count against the quota until it is resolved, and a confirmation after expiry must not turn its bytes into a confirmed object. **Decision:** the quota of an environment counts every object and every unconfirmed claim, expired ones included, until the claim is completed or erased; the claim and its quota guard are one batch. A confirmation after expiry is refused with no grace. The storage gateway's hourly sweep claims at most fifty unconfirmed claims a day old, oldest first, observes the object, waits for the thirty-second drain, replaces the payload with an erasure marker and completes the deletion with the marker proof. **Consequence:** an abandoned upload counts against the quota for up to a day plus one sweep; a provider answer for another object or a failed erasure stops only that claim.

## D496 Sampled operation hours are charged by their sample

<a id="d496"></a>

**State:** released to Development and Production on 14 November 2026. **Context:** A sampled hour is a lower bound of the requests that happened, so charging it as zero under-bills the usage. **Decision:** a sampled hour adds its sampled count to the charge, and is counted separately so repeated sampling stays visible. **Consequence:** the charge is a lower bound of the requests that happened when a sample was taken, never zero; the storage gauge is still not charged.

## D497 Canceled and CPU-limited invocations are billable

<a id="d497"></a>

**State:** released to Development and Production on 14 November 2026. **Context:** Canceled and CPU-limited invocations consume platform compute, so they are billable like the others. **Decision:** every `fetch` invocation that ran a script is billable whatever its outcome; `scriptNotFound` is the only outcome that never runs a script and is not billed. **Consequence:** billable request and CPU quantities rise for canceled and CPU-limited invocations; the Logpush source and the window rules are unchanged.

## D498 Schedules of credit-denied projects are disabled

<a id="d498"></a>

**State:** released to Development and Production on 14 November 2026. **Context:** A project whose organization is past its credit grace must not keep its database running at the platform's cost. **Decision:** the hourly sweep reads `listCreditDeniedProjects` in addition to `listStopBudgetProjects`: no renewal pending, balance minus open reservations at nothing, and either the grace lapsed or the month's overrun at the ceiling. Each list holds at most fifty projects in random order, each list is read independently, and a project in both is stopped once. The sweep runs even when billing fails. **Consequence:** a capped pass reaches every project over successive passes; re-enabling a schedule remains a manual action.

## D499 Re-drive of parked Stripe events within thirty days

<a id="d499"></a>

**State:** implemented in source; release and Stripe acceptance pending. **Context:** A parked event needs a defined re-drive, and its retries must back off instead of repeating every minute. **Decision:** any failure other than a provider outage waits one, two, four and so on minutes up to sixty before the next attempt. A parked event is claimed again with a fresh attempt while it was received less than thirty days ago, the period for which Stripe keeps an event. An older parked event is never claimed and stays for an operator, who lists it with the other rows that need a person through the maintenance read `listParkedRows`, which changes none of them. **Consequence:** an event Stripe may no longer deliver is never silently dropped, but an operator must act on rows older than thirty days.

## D500 Narrow PGCF entrypoint for the credit service

<a id="d500"></a>

**State:** released to Development and Production on 14 November 2026. **Context:** The credit service needs the usage collector and the schedule sweep only, and the full management entrypoint also serves database access. **Decision:** `NeonUsageService` exposes exactly `collectUsage` and `stopSchedules`; the Development, Production and local credit-service configurations and the release profile bind it, and the full entrypoint stays bound only to its own callers. **Consequence:** a compromise of the credit service reaches the hourly usage collector and the schedule sweep and nothing else of the Neon management surface.

## D624 Access that a Stripe coverage kept aside

<a id="d624"></a>

**State:** implemented in source (Core migrations 0811 and 0812); release and live acceptance pending. **Context:** A Stripe invoice that reached further than a granted or manual coverage replaced it, so ending the subscription cut granted days that Stripe never gave. A referral reward that arrived after such an overwrite never extended the kept access, so its clawback must not take days from it. **Decision:** the overwritten end is kept aside with its source and the instant it was kept (`granted_until`, `granted_source`, `granted_captured_at`). A subscription end or a full refund restores it when it runs past the Stripe coverage that remains. A referral clawback takes its thirty days off it only for a reward granted at or before the capture. An operator's coverage set by `setPaid` drops the kept access. **Consequence:** Stripe never cuts access it did not give. A reward granted while Stripe coverage was still running added nothing, so a later capture that follows a lapse and a new granted coverage can still lose its thirty days to that reward's clawback; the clawback of the Paid access approximates in the same way.

## D625 A customer intent without a customer starts a new attempt after a day

<a id="d625"></a>

**State:** implemented in source (Core migration 0900, BIL-03); release and live acceptance pending. **Context:** A Stripe customer intent that is a day old, has no provider identity and is not found by Stripe's metadata search refused every checkout, top-up, Paid subscription and recharge of its organization with a retryable outage forever, because its request id could not be used again. **Decision:** after the search finds no customer, the intent is renewed under a new request id, so the creation gets a fresh idempotency key. The renewal changes only the request id and creation instant of an intent without a provider identity, for an active organization, and only once the intent is a day old. A customer the search finds is adopted and never duplicated. **Consequence:** a Stripe customer that the search misses for a full day would be created a second time; the search is consistent far sooner than that. **Deploy order:** Core migrations 0900 and 0901 first, then the Core code, then the Billing Worker (see D626).

## D626 Deleted organizations end their Stripe billing once, and their Stripe payments are refused

<a id="d626"></a>

**State:** implemented in source (Core migration 0901, BIL-16); release and live acceptance pending. **Context:** A deleted WorkOS organization kept the Stripe subscription that Stripe bills, and a payment of its renewal was credited and extended its Paid coverage. **Decision:** the recharge pass that first sees an organization deleted opens a closure; it cancels each subscription of the customer that has not ended and detaches each card, at most twenty such changes per pass, and records the closure as ended only after Stripe accepted every one of them; an unfinished closure continues on a later pass. A closure is never reopened. A Stripe payment of a deleted organization is refused with `billing_organization_inactive`, so the event waits in the inbox for an operator; a replay of a payment that was credited before the deletion is acknowledged instead (`granted` false), and a suspended organization's payment is credited, since no refund is made. A write opens no credit account row for a deleted organization. **Consequence:** the owner decides parked payments of closed organizations; no refund is made automatically. **Deploy order:** Core migrations 0900 and 0901 first, then the Core code, then the Billing Worker, which refuses every call while its Core lacks a command.
